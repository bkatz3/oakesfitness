# What Is VO2 Max and Why Does It Matter More After 50?

*By The Oakes Fitness Team*

VO2 max, the maximum amount of oxygen your body can use during intense exercise, is one of the strongest predictors of how long you will live. In a study of 122,007 adults who underwent treadmill testing, people with the lowest cardiorespiratory fitness had a mortality risk worse than smokers or people with diabetes ([JAMA Network Open, 2018](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2707428)). Most people over 50 track steps. Almost nobody tracks the number that predicts survival better.

## What is VO2 max and why does it matter after 50?

VO2 max measures how efficiently your heart, lungs, and muscles deliver and use oxygen under maximum effort. It is the ceiling on your aerobic capacity, the point where your body simply runs out of gas.

In that same cohort, followed for a median of 8.4 years, adults with elite fitness had an 80 percent lower risk of death than those with the lowest fitness (adjusted hazard ratio 0.20). Low fitness carried more mortality risk than coronary artery disease (HR 1.29), smoking (HR 1.41), or diabetes (HR 1.40), and the benefit kept rising with no ceiling. Elite performers even outlived the merely "high fit" group ([JAMA Network Open, 2018](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2707428)).

That matters more after 50 because the decline is not linear. Researchers at the National Institute on Aging tracked 810 healthy adults ages 21 to 87 over a median of 7.9 years and found VO2 max drops around 3 to 6 percent per decade in your 20s and 30s, then accelerates past 20 percent per decade after 70, with a steeper slide in men starting in their 40s ([Circulation, 2005](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circulationaha.105.545459)). Your 50s sit right where the curve bends downward: max heart rate drops, stroke volume falls, and the aerobic buffer you never noticed in your 30s starts to disappear.

## Does strength training improve VO2 max?

Not much on its own. Strength training builds the muscle mass and joint resilience that let you move, but it is a poor stimulus for cardiorespiratory capacity. VO2 max responds to sustained and interval aerobic work that pushes your heart rate and breathing hard enough to challenge oxygen delivery. Heavy squats and presses train force production, not your heart's ability to pump more blood per beat.

This is why [Is Walking Enough Exercise After 50, or Do You Need to Lift?](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-06-29-walking-vs-lifting-longevity-after-50.html) is really two questions. Lifting protects muscle and bone. A dedicated aerobic stimulus protects your heart's capacity to deliver oxygen under demand. After 50, you need both.

## How do you train to improve VO2 max after 50?

Two types of sessions move the needle, and most people over 50 are doing neither.

| Method | What it looks like | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Zone 2 aerobic work | 30-45 minutes at a pace where you can still hold a conversation, 2-3x per week | Builds mitochondrial density and the aerobic base that supports higher efforts |
| Interval training | 4-6 rounds of 3-4 minutes hard, 3-4 minutes easy, once or twice per week | Directly challenges heart rate and stroke volume, the strongest driver of VO2 max gains |

Start conservatively, and progress intensity before duration. A rowing machine, incline treadmill walk, or stationary bike all work, since the goal is cardiovascular demand, not specific equipment. One client in his late 50s could sustain a brisk walk for only 10 minutes before his heart rate spiked into the 150s. Six months of weekly intervals layered onto his strength sessions and he was holding that same heart rate through a 30-minute row.

## Key Takeaways

- VO2 max, your body's maximum oxygen use under exertion, predicts long-term mortality better than smoking status or a diabetes diagnosis in a study of 122,007 adults.
- The lowest fitness group had roughly five times the mortality risk of the elite fitness group over a median 8.4-year follow-up.
- VO2 max decline accelerates with age, from about 3 to 6 percent per decade in your 20s and 30s to more than 20 percent per decade after 70.
- Strength training alone does not meaningfully improve VO2 max; it requires dedicated zone 2 and interval aerobic work.
- Two to three zone 2 sessions plus one to two interval sessions per week is enough to start reversing the trend after 50.
