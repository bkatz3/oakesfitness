# 10 Movements to Improve Your Grip Strength After 50

*By The Oakes Fitness Team*

Grip work after 50 gets treated as a hand gripper on the couch, or as something you skip because you already lift. Neither is a program. These ten movements cover the ways your hands actually fail (squeeze, pinch, support, and hanging) with doses a new lifter can use this week. [Why a stronger grip matters for how long you stay independent](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-06-03-why-grip-strength-predicts-how-long-youll-live-after-50) is a different article. This one is what to do.

## What Kinds of Grip Are You Training?

Four jobs, not one squeeze.

- **Support** is holding something at your side while you walk or stand: grocery bags, a suitcase, farmer carries.
- **Squeeze** is closing the hand: a gripper, a thick handle, a handshake.
- **Pinch** is thumb versus fingers: a plate, a book, a lid.
- **Hang** is your bodyweight on a bar or a towel, fingers doing the work of a hook.

Most gym programs train support by accident and ignore the rest. After 50, the tendons in the elbow and wrist also need the slower, smaller work, not only the heavy carries. If a movement lights up the inside or outside of the elbow, stop that variation and switch to a pain-free one. That is a programming change, not a reason to quit using your hands.

## What Are the 10 Movements?

Start with a load or a hold you can finish clean. Add time or weight after you own the current dose, not before.

| # | Movement | Grip job | Beginner dose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Farmer carry | Support | 20 to 40 second walks, 3 trips |
| 2 | Suitcase carry | Support, one side | 20 to 30 seconds each side, 3 trips |
| 3 | Dead hang | Hang | 10 to 20 seconds, 3 holds (toe on a box if needed) |
| 4 | Towel hang | Hang, squeeze | 8 to 15 seconds, 3 holds, feet on the floor at first |
| 5 | Gripper squeeze | Squeeze | 8 to 12 slow squeezes, 2 to 3 sets |
| 6 | Plate pinch | Pinch | 10 to 20 second holds, 3 per hand |
| 7 | Wrist curl | Forearm flexors | 10 to 12 reps, 2 sets, light dumbbell |
| 8 | Reverse wrist curl | Forearm extensors | 10 to 12 reps, 2 sets, lighter than the curl |
| 9 | Static bar hold | Support | 15 to 30 seconds, 3 holds |
| 10 | Band finger extension | Open-hand | 15 to 20 reps, 2 sets |

**1. Farmer carry.** A dumbbell or kettlebell in each hand, walked with tall posture and quiet shoulders. Pick a weight you can hold for the whole walk. If your fingers open at 12 seconds, the load is too heavy.

**2. Suitcase carry.** Same walk, one side only, then switch. The empty side has to stay stacked so you do not lean. This is the one that shows up when you carry a single bag of soil or a suitcase through an airport.

**3. Dead hang.** Hands on a pull-up bar, shoulders packed down, not shrugged into your ears. If a full hang is too much, keep one toe on a box and take as much weight as you can without pain. Build toward 20 to 30 seconds.

**4. Towel hang.** Drape a gym towel over the bar and grip the towel, not the steel. Harder than a bar hang, and closer to gripping a rope or a dog leash that wants to slide. Start with both feet on the floor and sit back into the towel.

**5. Gripper squeeze.** A hand gripper, or a rolled towel if you do not own one. Close it in 2 seconds, hold 2 seconds, open in 2 seconds. Fast pulses do not count. Stop a set or two before the elbow complains.

**6. Plate pinch.** Two iron plates, smooth sides out, pinched together with thumb on one face and fingers on the other. No bumper plates for this; they are too thick. Hold, set down quietly, switch hands. A single plate pinched by the rim works if you only have bumpers.

**7. Wrist curl.** Sit, forearm on your thigh, palm up, light dumbbell. Curl the wrist, lower under control. This is small-range work. Ego loading here is how people irritate the inside of the elbow.

**8. Reverse wrist curl.** Same setup, palm down, even lighter. The extensors on the back of the forearm are the ones that keep a mouse-and-keyboard elbow from taking over when you add carries.

**9. Static bar hold.** Hold a barbell, trap bar, or two heavy dumbbells at the top of a deadlift and do not walk. No extra pull. The job is keeping the bar in your hands. This is the gym version of holding a heavy object while you wait for a door.

**10. Band finger extension.** A thick rubber band around all five fingertips. Open the hand against the band and close slowly. Squeeze work without this is how forearms get imbalanced. Two sets at the end of a session is enough.

## How Do You Use These After 50 Without Wrecking Your Elbows?

Three movements per session, two or three days a week, after the main lifts. That is the whole template.

A workable week for a new lifter:

- **Day A:** Farmer carry, plate pinch, band finger extension
- **Day B:** Dead hang, wrist curl, reverse wrist curl
- **Day C:** Suitcase carry, towel hang, static bar hold

Leave the gripper off the days you already hung or carried heavy. [Tendons adapt slower than muscle after 50](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-05-27-why-tendons-age-differently-than-muscles), so add time or load on one movement at a time, not on all ten. A little lingering forearm fatigue the next day is fine. Sharp elbow pain during the set is the stop signal.

If you do not currently lift, put these around a [simple strength plan built for after 50](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-06-22-how-to-start-strength-training-after-50) rather than turning grip into the whole workout. Hands get stronger fastest when they have to hold real training, not when they only squeeze a gripper in the car.

## Key Takeaways

- Grip after 50 is four jobs (support, squeeze, pinch, hang), and a hand gripper only trains one of them.
- Farmer and suitcase carries, dead hangs, towel hangs, plate pinches, gripper squeezes, wrist curls, reverse wrist curls, static bar holds, and band finger extensions cover those jobs with beginner doses.
- Three movements per session, two or three days a week, after your main lifts, is enough; adding all ten in one day is how elbows get angry.
- Progress one movement at a time, because forearm tendons change slower than the muscle you feel the next morning.
- Sharp elbow or wrist pain during a variation means swap it, not push through it, and keep using the pain-free holds.
