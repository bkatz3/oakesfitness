# Should You Eat Before or After a Workout After 50?

*By The Oakes Fitness Team*

The internet splits this into two camps: you must eat the second you rack the last set, or you must train fasted if you want fat loss. Neither rule holds up after 50. Both eating before and eating after can work. What usually tanks progress is missing daily protein, or walking into a hard session empty enough that the session falls apart.

## Does a 30-Minute Window Decide Muscle After 50?

No. The so-called anabolic window is wider than a gym-floor timer, and total protein does more of the work than the stopwatch.

A 2013 review of nutrient-timing studies concluded that the post-workout "window" is not a 30-minute cliff, and that how close protein needs to sit to training depends on when you last ate ([Aragon and Schoenfeld, 2013, *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*, PMID 23360586](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23360586/)). Train after a real meal and amino acids are still in circulation. Train after an overnight fast and the next protein feeding matters because you have gone hours without protein, not because a magic hour expired.

A meta-analysis of 20 strength trials (478 people) and 23 hypertrophy trials (525 people) found that once total daily protein was in the model, timing protein immediately around the workout did not reliably beat eating it at other times of day. Total protein was the strongest predictor of the hypertrophy effect ([Schoenfeld, Aragon and Krieger, 2013, *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*, PMID 24299050](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24299050/)). The trials mixed ages. The "eat now or lose the session" claim still failed.

A 10-week randomized trial in 21 resistance-trained men compared 25 g of protein immediately before training with the same 25 g immediately after. Strength, muscle size, and body composition came out similar ([Schoenfeld et al., 2017, *PeerJ*, PMID 28070459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28070459/)). Those were younger trained men. Pre versus post is not a special lever even then. After 50, the lever that moves is a high enough protein dose at meals.

For how to space meals across the day, see [protein timing after 50](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-04-16-why-protein-timing-matters-more-after-50). This post is about the meal next to the workout.

## How Much Protein Should Sit Next to the Workout?

More than a younger lifter needs in one sitting, and less than a second dinner.

In 37 men averaging 71 years old, a leg workout plus 0, 10, 20, or 40 g of whey showed a clear dose pattern. At rest, 20 g of whey raised myofibrillar protein synthesis above fasting. After the exercise, 40 g beat 20 g. Younger muscle typically saturates near 20 g post-exercise; these older muscles did not ([Yang et al., 2012, *British Journal of Nutrition*, PMID 22313809](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22313809/)).

A pooled analysis of acute feeding studies found healthy older men (~71 years) needed about 0.40 g of protein per kilogram of body weight in a single bolus to plateau myofibrillar protein synthesis, versus about 0.24 g/kg in younger men (~22 years) ([Moore et al., 2015, *Journals of Gerontology Series A*, PMID 25056502](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25056502/)). For an 180-pound (82 kg) adult, that is roughly 33 g. Aim for 30 to 40 g of protein around training.

Daily totals still sit above that one meal. PROT-AGE recommended at least 1.0 to 1.2 g of protein per kilogram per day for older adults, and at least 1.2 g/kg for people who exercise ([Bauer et al., 2013, *Journal of the American Medical Directors Association*, PMID 23867520](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23867520/)). For a fuller daily target, see [how much protein you need after 50](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-02-25-how-much-protein-do-you-need-after-50-to-build-muscle).

Carbohydrate next to the session is a performance tool, not a hypertrophy law. Twenty to 40 g, 30 to 90 minutes before a hard lift, can keep the session from fading if you train early or get lightheaded. Skip a large, high-fat meal in the 20 minutes before squats.

## When Does Eating Before the Workout Help?

When session quality is the thing you would otherwise lose.

You can train fasted and still grow if the rest of the day covers protein. After 50, the more common failure is a weak first hour: dizziness, a pounding heart on warm-up sets, or cutting the last working sets because the tank is empty. That is a skipped stimulus, not a fat-loss win.

Eat 30 to 90 minutes before if you train early and feel shaky empty, if the session is heavy compounds, or if a big breakfast sits badly. If training and meals interact with blood-pressure or blood-sugar medication, ask the clinician who prescribed it.

A workable pre-session minimum: 20 to 30 g of protein and 20 to 40 g of carbohydrate, low fat, low fiber. Yogurt and a banana, toast with eggs, or a whey shake. Solid food 30 to 60 minutes prior; liquid 15 to 30 minutes.

If you use a later eating window, read [intermittent fasting after 50](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-05-02-intermittent-fasting-after-50) before pairing 16:8 with 5 a.m. lifting. Compressing meals can work. Doing both a fasted and under-fueled workout is a harder bet.

## When Does Eating After the Workout Matter?

When the next real meal is hours away, or when the last protein feeding was last night.

If you had a 30-to-40 g protein meal 1 to 3 hours before training, you are not on a countdown. Get a normal meal in within a few hours. If you trained fasted at 6 a.m. and lunch is at 1 p.m., eat 30 to 40 g of protein within about an hour of finishing, then eat lunch as planned.

Post-workout is also where people quietly under-eat for the day. A protein-centered meal after the session is insurance for the daily total, and it helps you [eat enough calories to support the work](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-03-30-how-to-eat-enough-calories-after-50).

You do not need an intra-workout shake for a 45-to-75 minute lift if you ate before or will eat soon after. Water covers most sessions.

## What Should You Eat Around Three Common Training Times?

Use this as a starting template this week. Adjust portions to body weight and stomach.

| When you train | Before | After | Why this version |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early morning (5–7 a.m.) | Fine fasted: coffee or tea, then train. If you fade: 20–30 g protein + 20–40 g carbs 15–60 min prior (whey + banana). | 30–40 g protein within about an hour (eggs, yogurt, leftover chicken, or a shake). | Overnight fast is long; session quality and first protein dose both matter. |
| Midday (11 a.m.–1 p.m.) | Normal meal 1–3 hours before: 30–40 g protein, mixed carbs. | If that meal already hit 30–40 g protein, wait for the next meal. If it was light, add 20–30 g protein after. | Already fed; do not stack two huge meals. |
| Evening (5–7 p.m.) | Snack 60–90 min prior if dinner will be late: 20–30 g protein. Or train after a normal afternoon meal. | Dinner with 30–40 g protein within a couple of hours. Do not skip dinner because you had a shake. | Risk is going to bed short on daily protein, not missing a 30-minute window. |

A 180-pound adult aiming near 1.2 g/kg needs about 100 g of protein across the day. Three meals of about 35 g, with the workout next to one of them, beats perfect shaker timing and 60 g total. If mornings stay miserable even with a small feed, train later.

## Key Takeaways

- Eating immediately before a workout and eating immediately after can both support muscle after 50; neither is required in a 30-minute window if daily protein is high enough.
- A meta-analysis of resistance-training trials found total protein intake predicted hypertrophy better than whether protein was timed right next to the session.
- In men averaging 71 years old, 40 g of whey after lifting raised muscle protein synthesis more than 20 g, a dose that usually saturates younger muscle.
- A practical peri-workout protein target after 50 is 30 to 40 g of high-quality protein, which lines up with about 0.40 g per kilogram of body weight in a single feeding in older men.
- Eat something before the session if you train early, get lightheaded, or lose set quality when fasted; skip a large, high-fat meal in the 20 minutes before heavy lifting.
- Eat after the session if the next meal is hours away or you trained after an overnight fast; if you already ate 1 to 3 hours before, a normal meal within a few hours is enough.
- PROT-AGE recommended at least 1.2 g of protein per kilogram per day for older adults who exercise; hit that across the day, then let workout timing follow your schedule and stomach.
