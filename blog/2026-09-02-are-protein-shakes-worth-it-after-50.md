# Are Protein Shakes Worth It After 50, or Is Food Enough?

*By The Oakes Fitness Team*

You either need a shake after every lift or shakes are junk and only whole food counts. Neither rule holds after 50. Muscle responds to enough high-quality protein plus training, not to a blender. Food is enough when you hit the target. A shake is useful when appetite, schedule, or chewing gets in the way of that target.

## What Actually Drives Muscle After 50?

Total daily protein and resistance training do the heavy lifting. Timing and source matter less than whether each meal clears a higher dose threshold than it did at 30.

Aging muscle shows anabolic resistance: the same protein dose that raises muscle protein synthesis in a younger adult often under-stimulates older muscle. A pooled analysis of acute feeding studies found healthy older men (~71 years) needed about 0.40 g of protein per kilogram of body weight in a single bolus to plateau myofibrillar protein synthesis, versus about 0.24 g/kg in younger men (~22 years) ([Moore et al., 2015, *Journals of Gerontology Series A*, PMID 25056502](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25056502/)). For an 180-pound (82 kg) adult, that is roughly 33 g per meal. Aim for 30 to 40 g.

Daily totals sit above any one meal. The PROT-AGE Study Group recommended at least 1.0 to 1.2 g of protein per kilogram per day for older adults, and at least 1.2 g/kg for people who exercise ([Bauer et al., 2013, *Journal of the American Medical Directors Association*, PMID 23867520](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23867520/)). For a 180-pound adult who lifts, that is about 100 g across the day, often closer to 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg if you are training hard. For the full daily range, see [how much protein you need after 50](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-02-25-how-much-protein-do-you-need-after-50-to-build-muscle). For how to space it, see [protein timing after 50](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-04-16-why-protein-timing-matters-more-after-50).

## Does a Shake Build More Muscle Than Food?

Not when the protein dose and quality are matched. Whole food can raise muscle protein synthesis in older adults without a powder.

In healthy young (n=17; ~34 years) and older (n=17; ~68 years) adults, a 113 g serving of lean beef (~30 g protein) raised mixed muscle protein synthesis by about 50 percent in both age groups. Tripling the serving to 90 g of protein did not raise synthesis further ([Symons et al., 2009, *Journal of the American Dietetic Association*, PMID 19699838](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19699838/)). Food protein, at a moderate serving, was enough to maximally stimulate synthesis in that design.

Whey earns its reputation for speed and leucine, not for magic. In 37 men averaging 71 years, a leg workout plus 0, 10, 20, or 40 g of whey showed a clear dose pattern: after exercise, 40 g beat 20 g for myofibrillar protein synthesis ([Yang et al., 2012, *British Journal of Nutrition*, PMID 22313809](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22313809/)). In 48 older men (~74 years), 20 g of whey raised postprandial mixed muscle protein synthesis more than the same amount of casein or casein hydrolysate, linked to faster absorption and higher leucine ([Pennings et al., 2011, *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, PMID 21367943](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21367943/)).

Those studies compare protein types and doses. They do not prove a shake beats an equal gram count from chicken, eggs, yogurt, or fish. A large meta-analysis of 49 resistance-training trials (1,863 people) found protein supplementation increased gains in strength and fat-free mass during training, with a smaller average effect as age rose, and little extra benefit once total intake reached about 1.6 g/kg/day ([Morton et al., 2018, *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, PMID 28698222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)). Supplements helped most when they closed a gap in total protein, not because powder is inherently superior to matched food.

## When Does a Shake Help After 50?

When it is the difference between hitting 100-plus grams and finishing the day at 60.

Common gaps after 50: a small breakfast, a busy morning with no time to cook, reduced appetite after a hard session, dental issues that make steak unpleasant, or a long gap between lunch and dinner. A shake is scaffolding for those hours. It is not required if three solid meals already deliver 30 to 40 g of protein each.

Use a shake when:

| Situation | Why a shake fits | Food-first alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Early training, no appetite for eggs | Liquid goes down faster; 25–35 g protein in minutes | Greek yogurt + milk if that sits better |
| Lunch is a meeting and a bag of chips | Protects the midday 30–40 g dose | Deli turkey, cottage cheese, or leftover chicken |
| Chewing steak or chicken is hard | Soft protein without skipping the day | Eggs, yogurt, flaked fish, soft tofu |
| Dinner is late and the day is short | Adds grams without another full plate | Cottage cheese or a second dairy serving |

If the question is what to eat next to the workout itself, that is covered in [should you eat before or after a workout after 50](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-09-01-should-you-eat-before-or-after-a-workout-after-50). This post is about whether powder belongs in the day at all.

## How Should You Choose and Use One?

Keep it simple. Look for a product that delivers about 25 to 35 g of protein per serving with a short ingredient list. Whey isolate or concentrate works for most people who tolerate dairy. If dairy sits badly, a soy isolate or a blended plant protein can work if the serving still lands near 30 g and you are willing to use a slightly larger scoop when leucine is lower. Casein digests more slowly and suits a later snack better than a rush between meetings.

Mix with water or milk. Add fruit if you need carbohydrate around training. Do not treat the shake as a second dessert with syrups and cookie crumbs if body composition is a goal. And do not skip real meals because you had a shake; under-eating calories still undercuts training after 50 ([how to eat enough calories after 50](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-03-30-how-to-eat-enough-calories-after-50)).

A workable day for a 180-pound adult aiming near 100 to 120 g:

- Breakfast: eggs and Greek yogurt (~35 g) or a whey shake if mornings are empty (~30 g)
- Lunch: chicken or tuna plate (~35 g)
- Post-workout or mid-afternoon: shake only if lunch was light (~25–30 g)
- Dinner: fish, lean beef, or tofu with sides (~35–40 g)

Two food meals plus one shake can beat three weak meals. Four food meals with no powder can beat living on shakes. Count the grams.

This is nutrition context for training, not medical advice. If you have kidney disease, use protein changes only under clinician guidance.

## Key Takeaways

- Food is enough after 50 when daily protein and per-meal doses are high enough; a shake does not build more muscle than matched high-quality food protein.
- PROT-AGE recommended at least 1.2 g of protein per kilogram per day for older adults who exercise, which is about 100 g for an 180-pound adult who lifts.
- Older muscle often needs about 0.40 g of protein per kilogram in a single meal to maximize myofibrillar protein synthesis, roughly 30 to 40 g for many adults.
- A moderate serving of lean beef (~30 g protein) raised muscle protein synthesis about 50 percent in both young and older adults, showing whole food can fully stimulate the response.
- Whey is a convenient, leucine-rich option that raises synthesis in older men in a dose-dependent way after lifting, and 20 g of whey outperformed the same dose of casein in one older-men trial.
- Protein supplements help resistance-training gains mainly by raising total intake; beyond roughly 1.6 g/kg/day, extra powder adds little.
- Use a shake when appetite, time, or chewing blocks a 30-to-40 g meal, not as a rule for every workout.
