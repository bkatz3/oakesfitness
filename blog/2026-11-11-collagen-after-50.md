# Is Collagen Worth Taking After 50 for Joints and Muscle?

*By The Oakes Fitness Team*

Collagen gets sold as the protein your joints and muscles are missing after 50. That oversells it. The trials show small help for joint pain and some help for muscle when it is added to lifting, but most of them compared collagen with nothing, not with eating enough of a better protein. For muscle after 50, that second comparison is the one that matters.

## What Is Collagen Supposed to Do?

Collagen is the main structural protein in tendons, ligaments, cartilage, skin, and bone. Supplements are usually collagen peptides (also called hydrolyzed collagen) or gelatin, made from animal hide, bone, or fish.

The pitch is simple: you lose collagen with age, so eat collagen and put it back. Digestion does not work that way. Collagen is broken into amino acids and small peptides like any other protein, and your body decides where they go. So the useful questions are narrower. Does collagen ease joint pain more than a placebo? And is it good protein for muscle compared with the alternatives?

## Does Collagen Help Joint Pain?

A little, in some studies. The size of the effect is the part the label leaves out.

In a 24-week trial at Penn State, 147 college athletes with activity-related joint pain and no diagnosed joint disease took 10 g of collagen hydrolysate or a placebo every day ([Clark et al., 2008, *Current Medical Research and Opinion*, PMID 18416885](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18416885/)). Only 97 of them made it into the analysis. The collagen group reported somewhat bigger drops in pain while walking, standing, lifting, carrying, and at rest. On the study's pain scales, the gap between groups was roughly half a point.

A German trial gave 139 young athletes with knee pain during activity either 5 g of collagen peptides or a placebo daily for 12 weeks ([Zdzieblik et al., 2017, *Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism*, PMID 28177710](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28177710/)). Pain during activity fell more with collagen than with placebo, by about 4 to 6 points on the study's pain scale. Pain at rest did not differ between groups, and knee range of motion did not change significantly.

Both of those were young athletes, not adults over 50. For osteoarthritis, a meta-analysis of placebo-controlled trials found collagen improved total scores on the WOMAC, a standard osteoarthritis questionnaire, along with stiffness and visual analogue scale scores ([García-Coronado et al., 2019, *International Orthopaedics*, PMID 30368550](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30368550/)). The WOMAC pain and physical function subscores did not change significantly. The authors called collagen effective. Read more carefully, the benefit is real on some measures and absent on the two most people care about.

None of these trials shows collagen rebuilding cartilage. For what loading does to cartilage, see [why cartilage wears down faster after 50](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-10-14-why-cartilage-wears-down-faster-after-50).

## Does Collagen Help Your Body Make Connective Tissue?

Possibly, when it is timed around activity, but the evidence is early.

In a small crossover study, 8 healthy men took 5 g or 15 g of vitamin C-enriched gelatin or a placebo, then skipped rope for 6 minutes an hour later, three times a day for three days ([Shaw et al., 2017, *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, PMID 27852613](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27852613/)). With 15 g taken an hour before exercise, a blood marker of new collagen production doubled.

That is a blood marker in eight men, not stronger tendons in a 60-year-old. Tendons adapt to load over months, and that slow timeline is covered in [why tendons age differently than muscles](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-05-27-why-tendons-age-differently-than-muscles).

## Does Collagen Build Muscle After 50?

This is where the marketing and the trials split.

The most cited trial put 53 men averaging 72 years old, all with age-related muscle loss (sarcopenia), through 12 weeks of supervised resistance training three times a week ([Zdzieblik et al., 2015, *British Journal of Nutrition*, DOI 10.1017/S0007114515002810](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114515002810)). Half took 15 g of collagen peptides after training and half took a placebo. Both groups improved, because both were lifting. The collagen group gained more fat-free mass (4.2 kg vs 2.9 kg), lost more fat (5.5 kg vs 3.5 kg), and gained more quadriceps strength (16.1 Nm vs 7.4 Nm).

The detail that matters is the placebo. It was silicon dioxide, which contains no protein. So the trial shows that adding 15 g of protein to training beat adding nothing. It does not show that collagen beats the same amount of whey, eggs, or Greek yogurt.

That head-to-head test came later. In a randomized trial of 22 healthy women averaging 69, one group took 30 g of whey and the other 30 g of collagen, twice a day for six days, while training one leg ([Oikawa et al., 2020, *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, PMID 31919527](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31919527/)). Whey raised muscle protein synthesis in both the rested leg and the trained leg. Collagen raised it acutely only in the trained leg, and across the six days it did not rise significantly above baseline in either. Whey beat collagen in both legs.

The reason shows up in the amino acids. Per serving, the whey in that trial had 4.3 g of leucine and 15.4 g of essential amino acids. The collagen had 0.9 g of leucine and 5.6 g of essential amino acids. Leucine is the amino acid most tied to switching on muscle protein synthesis, which is why our guide to [how much protein you need after 50](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-02-25-how-much-protein-do-you-need-after-50-to-build-muscle) focuses on it. Collagen is a weak source.

## Collagen vs Getting Enough Protein: Where Does Each Fit?

| Goal | What collagen showed | Better first move |
|---|---|---|
| Build or keep muscle | Beat a no-protein placebo with training; lost to whey head-to-head | Hit your daily protein from leucine-rich foods and lift 2 to 3 times a week |
| Joint pain with activity | Small improvements in young athletes | Strengthen the muscles around the joint and adjust load |
| Osteoarthritis symptoms | Better total score and stiffness; pain and function subscores unchanged | Train around the joint consistently (see [exercising with arthritis](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-09-17-should-you-exercise-with-arthritis)) |
| Tendon health | A blood marker doubled in 8 young men | Slow, progressive loading over months |

## How to Use Collagen If You Still Want to Try It

1. **Count protein first.** Our [protein shake guide](https://oakesfitness.com/blog/2026-09-02-are-protein-shakes-worth-it-after-50) lands on about 30 to 40 g of protein per meal for most adults over 50. Get it from eggs, fish, poultry, dairy, meat, soy, or whey.
2. **Do not count collagen toward that number for muscle.** Treat it as extra on top of your meals, not a swap for a serving of whey or chicken.
3. **For joints, use what the trials used.** That was 5 to 10 g a day for 12 to 24 weeks. Give it about three months, then judge it by whether pain during activity changed.
4. **If you take it around training,** the one protocol with data used 15 g of vitamin C-enriched gelatin an hour before activity. Treat that as reasonable but unproven.
5. **Keep lifting.** In the sarcopenia trial, the placebo group still gained almost 3 kg of fat-free mass from training alone.

If a doctor has limited your protein intake for a medical reason, check with them before adding any protein supplement.

## Key Takeaways

- In 53 older men with sarcopenia, 15 g of collagen a day plus 12 weeks of resistance training produced more fat-free mass and strength than training with a placebo that contained no protein.
- When collagen was tested against an equal dose of whey in women averaging 69, whey raised muscle protein synthesis more in both rested and trained legs, and collagen did not raise it significantly over six days.
- Collagen is low in leucine and essential amino acids, which makes it a weak protein for building muscle compared with whey, eggs, dairy, or meat.
- Joint pain trials in young athletes found collagen lowered activity-related pain slightly more than placebo, by roughly half a point on the pain scales in one 24-week trial.
- An osteoarthritis meta-analysis found better total WOMAC and stiffness scores with collagen, but no significant change in the pain or function subscores.
- Evidence that collagen helps build tendon or ligament comes mostly from a blood marker study in eight young men, so loading over months remains the proven driver.
- After 50, getting about 30 to 40 g of protein per meal and lifting consistently matters more for muscle than adding collagen.
